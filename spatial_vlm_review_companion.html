Arc 0 · before the models

Cognition supplies the ontology

Four decades of spatial cognition — Marr's levels, Newcombe's three kinds, cognitive-map research — define what a spatial benchmark could measure. The LLM literature inherits this vocabulary without inheriting its methodology.

Arc 1 · 2023

Diagnosis: it's the data

First-generation probes ask whether transformers handle spatial relations at all. They don't — 56% vs 99% human. The verdict blames the pre-training corpus rather than the architecture, which sets the 2024 agenda.

Arc 2 · 2024

Synthesize data, graph the world

Two responses run in parallel: manufacture the missing supervision (SpatialVLM, ZeroVLM, EmbSpatial-SFT), and restructure the model around graphs — scene graphs for objects, topological graphs for routes. Fine-tuning beats chain-of-thought, but human-level gaps persist.

Arc 3 · 2025

Complexity, time, and bodies

Benchmarks stop asking "can it?" and start varying difficulty — by scale (SPACE), by composition (SPHERE), by input complexity (ChartBench). Simultaneously the field goes egocentric and temporal, and spills into geospatial and robotics applications where failures cost something.

Arc 4 · 2025–26

Turning inward

With performance ceilings visible, attention shifts from scores to mechanisms: attention-focus analyses, counterfactual perturbation, sparse autoencoders. Video temporal grounding splits off as its own field. This is the opening the review argues benchmark design should exploit.
